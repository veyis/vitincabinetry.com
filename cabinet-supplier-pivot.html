Cabinet Supplier Pivot

Project: vitrincabinetry.com Date: 2026-05-12 Status: 9 of 9 sections drafted Approach: C — Two-Tier Cabinet House

The pivot, in one paragraph

The current Vitrin site is built around "Designed, built, and installed by our own hands." That sentence is also the one that scares contractors away — it claims the whole-job revenue that contractors live on. The client has decided to drop the design-build-install framing and reposition Vitrin as a Quakertown cabinet supplier: Vitrin Stock from the showroom for the buyer who needs cabinets this week, and Vitrin Signature custom-built kitchens for the buyer who wants a designed space. Installation stays available on request, but never as the headline. The trade channel becomes a first-class audience served by the same brand.

"Most local kitchen shops are reselling a manufactured cabinet line and subcontracting installation. Vitrin will be the rare thing — a real manufacturer that also stocks, sells across the counter, and gets out of the contractor's way."

The industry pattern this borrows from

Dual-channel cabinet supply is rare in the residential market but well-understood in adjacent categories. The pivot quietly imports the playbook from:

  • Floor & Decor Pro — One physical store, two doors (retail + pro desk), separate pricing tiers, transparent inventory.
  • Sherwin-Williams — Same building, retail counter and pro counter, dedicated reps, net terms.
  • Home Depot Pro — Pro Xtra loyalty + bulk pricing + delivery infrastructure visible to both audiences.
  • IKEA Business — Same product line, audience-segmented marketing and CTAs at every step.

None of Vitrin's local competitors run this play. The opportunity in strategy.md ("the quadrant nobody owns") was Quakertown-local + true custom. The pivot moves the flag: Quakertown-local + dual-channel cabinet supply. Larger TAM, lower-trust friction, harder for competitors to copy fast.

5 · Installation page locked

The trickiest page in the entire pivot. It must be (a) findable for the homeowner who needs install, (b) quiet enough that contractors don't read it as competition, (c) honest about pricing, (d) routed back into the existing custom-kitchen funnel without spinning up a parallel sales process.

Page structure

  1. Hero — "Installation, if you want it."
    Subhead: "Most of our cabinets are installed by the contractor who bought them. For homeowners buying direct, we offer installation as an optional service."
    Photo: a Vitrin installer hands-on with cabinets — not a kitchen reveal photo.
  2. Who handles install (table — the contractor-reassurance moment):
    Buyer typeTypically installed by
    Homeowner buying directVitrin install crew, or homeowner's chosen contractor
    Contractor / installerTheir own crew
    Designer / architectTheir preferred install partner
    BuilderTheir on-site crew

    Three of four buyer types install with their own crew. The homeowner is the exception. This table is the page's most important block.

  3. What's included — Cabinet install only. Filler/scribe fitting. Crown / light rail. Toe kick. Hardware install. Punch list. Counter templating coordination (not templating itself).
  4. What's NOT included — Plumbing, electrical, tile, drywall, flooring. We coordinate with your trades but don't perform them. This line draws the boundary so contractors don't read "install" as "GC services."
  5. Pricing model — "Installation is typically 10–15% of cabinet order value for kitchens, 8–12% for bath. Final figure included in your quote."
  6. Timeline — Most installs scheduled within 2–4 weeks of cabinet delivery. Crew of 2–3. Most kitchens installed in 5–10 working days.
  7. CTA — single CTA: "Add installation to your cabinet quote" → /contact?type=install. Not a separate sales funnel.

Page is intentionally short — one screen of content. No portfolio. No big photos. No process flow. The opposite of the /process page energy. The page exists to inform, not to sell.

7 · Brand voice & language rules locked

The pivot lives or dies in word choice. A single misplaced word like "consultation" in the homepage CTA signals "design firm" and undoes everything else. This section is the editor's bible — give it to anyone writing copy for the site.

Vocabulary

Drop (never use)Use instead
Design-build / Design-build-installCabinet supplier · Cabinet shop
Design partner · Behind your nameSupply partner · Trade partner
Your project · Your remodel (in headlines)Your cabinets · Your order
Consultation (as the only CTA)Quote · Showroom visit · Pricing
Turnkey kitchen · Whole-home remodelCustom kitchen · Stock cabinets

Custom-built kitchens for youCustom kitchens, built at our bench
Lifetime kitchen warrantyLifetime cabinet warranty
Single-point promise · One company, one team (as ownership claims)One supplier, one rep, one accountable shop

Headline rules

  1. Lead with the noun (Cabinets). Never with the verb of service ("We design," "We build," "We install").
  2. Use "and" not "or" for dual-channel signaling. ("Cabinets, built and sold in Quakertown.")
  3. Geographic anchor in ~90% of headlines. Quakertown works — keep it.
  4. Avoid sentimental kitchen-reveal language ("heart of the home," "where memories are made"). Suppliers don't write that way; design firms do.

Voice attributes

  • Plainspoken, not poetic. "Stock cabinets, ready to take home." Not "Crafted with care for the home you've always dreamed of."
  • Numbers when possible. "4–8 weeks." "10–15% of cabinet order." Numbers signal supply discipline.
  • Transactional verbs. Buy. Pick up. Order. Quote. Ship. Deliver. Stock. Supply.
  • Trade-respectful. Never describe Vitrin doing something the trade audience does for themselves. "We'll install for you" is bad. "We install if you want, or your contractor can" is good.
  • No emojis. No exclamation marks. Suppliers do not use exclamation marks.

Audience-respecting copy patterns

  • Every retail-facing page gets a small trade band: "Buying for trade? See trade pricing →"
  • Every trade-facing page gets the inverse, optional: "Not a trade buyer? Visit our showroom →"
  • Photograph products, not outcomes. Cabinets ready to ship beats finished-kitchen reveals on the supplier-facing pages. (Outcome photography is still right for /portfolio and /cabinets/custom.)

9 · Adjacent operational considerations flagged, not in scope

These items are adjacent to the website pivot but critical for the pivot to work in practice. They're not part of the website spec — they're an operational checklist for the client. Flagged here so nothing falls through.

ItemWhy it mattersRecommended action
Photography Without supplier-side photos (will-call counter, stock shelf, jobsite delivery, contractor at the counter) the "supplier" signal stays weak. Today's photo library is all finished-kitchen reveals — those photos read as design firm. Half-day photo shoot once showroom address is set. Shot list: 8–10 supplier-side shots, plus 4–5 outcome shots for portfolio.
Trade application infrastructure The trade page promises a 2-minute apply → 2-day pricing sheet flow. Without a real form + CRM sequence, the promise breaks on the first lead. HubSpot or Tally form → Mailchimp/Klaviyo sequence: welcome → pricing sheet PDF → first-order incentive. Owner: client (not a code task).
Credit / account terms infrastructure "Account terms after first paid order" requires a credit application, optional D&B check, and AR follow-up. Otherwise the promise is empty. Set up Quickbooks or Xero AR workflow. Build a credit application PDF. Decide Net 15 vs Net 30 internally.
Inventory truth "Stock cabinets in stock" must be real. A buyer driving to Quakertown to find an out-of-stock cabinet ends the brand. Airtable, Google Sheets, or POS-driven inventory list. Mid-term: surface live inventory on /cabinets/stock via API.
Pricing sheet design The pricing sheet IS the trade pitch in physical form. A bad PDF undoes the whole site. 2-page branded PDF. Tier pricing visible. Updated quarterly. Sent only to approved trade applicants.
Dedicated trade rep The "one dedicated rep" promise requires a real person. If the client doesn't have a rep yet, the page needs softer language. Hire / designate. Until then: rewrite "one dedicated rep" → "a direct line to our trade desk."
Email segmentation Trade leads and homeowner leads should never receive the same nurture sequence. Mixing audiences hurts both. Mailchimp/Klaviyo lists split by audience: Homeowner Leads / Trade Leads / Past Customers / Designers. Different sequences per audience.
Showroom physical setup The site implies stock cabinets are visible and ready in the showroom. If the physical space is bare or all-custom-displays, the supplier story fails in person. Display a wall of stock cabinets when the showroom opens. Will-call counter visible. Trade-pricing-sheet stand by the door.
Hours that match contractor schedules Current site lists 9-6 weekday hours. Contractors pick up at 7am. A pickup window of 7-8am, Mon-Fri, is a structural advantage that the homepage should mention. Decide if 7am pickup-by-appointment is operationally possible. If yes, advertise it on /trade and /cabinets/stock.
